# Castellum Rotae v2: proposal (mockup stage, not approved)

What changes from v0.1.0, for review together with the mockup frames in `build/siege_mock/`.

## Look

- Presentation `2d` at 960 x 540 art pixels (x2 at 1080p): isometric pixel art in the style of the reference, every asset drawn by our own code (no fal, nothing copied).
- The art comes from a small isometric rasterizer in GDScript: boxes, beams, cylinders and terrain with real sun shadows, procedural textures (masonry, planks, tiles, rock strata, gold veins, water) and a dithered tone ramp per material, which gives the limited palette. Tiny sprites (people about 5 px tall, so only the head reads), palms and effects are depth-tested against the same depth buffer.
- In the game: the static world is baked once into a colour and a depth texture (generated by a tool from code and committed, because baking takes minutes in GDScript). Moving things (wheels, catapult arm, ballista, pump beam, stamp mill, gates, enemies, projectiles, tar) are frame strips or sprites rendered by the same rasterizer and drawn with a depth-test shader against the baked depth, so they tuck behind walls correctly. Tilt-shift and depth of field are a screen shader with the fort in the sharp band (an option, on by default, as in Diesel Domestiques).
- The fort is built against a mountain with gold veins; a moat runs around the other three sides; one bridge crosses it to the only gate. Three dry dirt roads meet at the bridgehead. Attackers are Egyptian-inspired (linen, dark hair, hide shields, fan standards, chariots), with no real insignia. Defenders are Romans in madder tunics. Player colours stay the only saturated hues.

## Fort and power

- Four rotae (one per rider, bots in empty ones), each built differently: a drum treadwheel (red), a ladder wheel with outside rungs and cross braces (blue), a wheel with a bronze inner ring (yellow), and an iron-banded paddle wheel (green). The near rim is painted in the rider's colour and each wheel flies the rider's pennant. Walkers inside: one to three by watts, as today.
- The junction is a visible crossbar: the four input shafts run south, the five output buses run east-west, and a clutch box sits at every crossing. The engaged clutch is bronze with its lever up; the shaft from the wheel to the clutch and the bus from the clutch to the station glow in the rider's colour and scroll at a speed proportional to P_eff. Changing station = moving your clutch (the 2 s reconnect stays, shown as the lever travelling).

## Stations (rider caps)

| Station | Cap | Role | Physics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gold mine | 2 | Economy (replaces the generator): gold per kJ of P_eff; carts, stamp mill and bucket chain run with the watts | none |
| Ballista | 1 | Single heavy bolt; pierces in a line; best against siege towers and rams | flat fast flight (about 75 m/s), lead-aimed; misses only what changes direction |
| Catapult | 2 | Stone with 4 m splash: area damage on infantry blocks, heavy damage to siege engines | high arc, 2-3 s flight, lead-aimed at the predicted spot; fast chariots often leave the splash |
| Arrow battery | 2 | Volleys of about 20 arcing arrows with spread; many small hits; weak against armour | each arrow flies and hits what is within 0.6 m where it lands |
| Tar pump | 1 | The shield: a tar curtain over the gate with its own HP that soaks gate damage first, refilled by watts; the coat on the bridge slows everything on it by 50% | tar flows and drips in the view only |

- Caps add up to 8 for 4 riders, so the team can never run everything at once: every switch is a choice, and in the mockup frame the gold mine stands idle while the four riders hold the weapons and the tar.
- Output per station stays `m = sum(P_eff x od) / 250 W`, capped at 4.0 (Overdrive unchanged).
- Build pads: four marble plinths on the wall towers. They hold crew-wound weapons bought with gold (a scorpion today); they fire slowly on their own and need no rider. Upgrades on stations and pads use gold, as do gate repair and Iron bands.

## Projectiles (rules)

- Shots are deterministic projectiles in the rules, at the 0.05 s step, with gravity and quadratic drag and the seeded RNG for spread. Weapons aim by solving the intercept for the target's predicted road position, so hits and misses come from the flight.
- Damage lands on impact: a bolt sweeps its path (pierce), a stone hits everything within its splash radius, an arrow hits the nearest enemy within 0.6 m.
- Clients replay each shot from a `shot` event (tick, station, origin, launch velocity, seed), so the snapshot does not grow.

## Enemies

Infantry blocks (spears), shield bearers (armoured), archers, chariots (fast), battering rams (they batter the tar curtain first, then the gate) and siege towers. A siege tower that reaches the moat edge throws its bridge across and unloads infantry onto the wall, which stops the nearest station until they are cleared.

## Safety and the rest

Nothing changes in the effort plans, ERG targets, Overdrive's SIM block or the BRIEF 4.6 checks: this redesign touches the presentation and the combat rules only. Waves, breaks, shop timing, score and XP keep their shape; their numbers get rebalanced once the new combat runs in the sims.
